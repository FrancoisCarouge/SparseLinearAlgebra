Definition utility.hpp:987

The structural pattern of the Cholesky factor L of a symmetric positive definite matrix A = L * transposed(L), from the pattern of A.

The factor is lower triangular. It stores the diagonal, the positions of the lower triangle of the pattern, and the positions the elimination fills in. The factor L of A = L * D * transposed(L), of a diagonal D, has the same pattern. A position is stored even if its value cancels out at run time. The factorization of a positive definite matrix does not pivot: unlike that of a division, the pattern of the factor does not depend on the values of the matrix.

The pattern is structurally symmetric and stores its diagonal: a positive definite matrix is symmetric and has a positive diagonal, which a structural zero can never hold.

Note
The rows and the columns keep the order of the pattern: no permutation reduces the fill-in, so the order of the rows and the columns decides it. The same pattern factors without fill-in when its densest rows and columns come last, and fills the whole lower triangle when they come first.

The structural pattern of a matrix division, the quotient x of x * rhs = lhs, by a square denominator of full structural rank.

A position is stored whenever the solution reaches it from a stored element of the numerator: a diagonal denominator keeps the numerator's pattern, a triangular one closes it along its substitution, and an irreducible diagonal block of the denominator's block triangular form fills every row of the numerator it reaches. A position is stored even if its value cancels out at run time.

The structural rank of a pattern: the largest count of its positions no two of which share a row or a column.

The rank of a matrix is at most the structural rank of its pattern, and equal to it for almost every value of its stored elements. A square matrix of a pattern of a structural rank below its size is singular, whatever the values of its stored elements.
